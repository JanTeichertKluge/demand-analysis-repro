\section{Robustness Check: Embeddings without Fine-Tuning}\label{app:gemma}

\revised{This section repeats the analysis with off-the-shelf embeddings that are not fine-tuned. The workflow is exactly the same as in the main analysis (Section~\ref{app:workflow}), except that the fine-tuning in step~2 is omitted, the embeddings in step~3 are computed with EmbeddingGemma~2 \citep{embeddinggemma2}, and their truncation to 256 coordinates replaces the random projection in Algorithm~\ref{alg:features}. All other steps of Algorithm~\ref{alg:features}, the sample split, the estimation sample, the tabular controls, the learners, and the random seeds are unchanged.}

\revised{EmbeddingGemma~2 is an open multimodal embedding model released by Google DeepMind in October 2026. Built on Gemma 4, it maps text, images, video, and audio into a shared 768-dimensional space. Of its 740M parameters, we use the 270M-parameter text model and the 170M-parameter vision encoder. The description and the image of each product are encoded jointly, with the task prefix for clustering (checkpoint \texttt{google/embeddinggemma-2}, revision \texttt{914f7f8}). As the model is trained with Matryoshka representation learning, we keep the leading 256 coordinates of each embedding and re-normalize them. Unlike the fine-tuned embeddings, these embeddings are not trained on our data and use neither tabular inputs nor the price and quantity signals. The comparison therefore changes the encoders, their inputs, and the fine-tuning at the same time. Figures~\ref{fig:gemma_ate} and \ref{fig:gemma_sorted} and Table~\ref{tab:gemma_chi2} also show two variants: text-only embeddings (256 dimensions) and text and image embeddings with all 768 dimensions.}

\paragraph{Clusters.} \revised{Figure~\ref{fig:gemma_clusters} shows the four products closest to each of the five cluster centers: toddler construction-truck sets, 1:64 scale die-cast cars, fingerboards, toy train sets, and larger-scale die-cast model cars. Over all 7,549 products, these clusters are more closely aligned with the 11 product subcategories than the fine-tuned clusters (normalized mutual information 0.29 vs.\ 0.06), and the two cluster assignments differ substantially (adjusted Rand index 0.06).}

\paragraph{Prediction.} \revised{Table~\ref{tab:gemma_r2} compares the test $R^2$ of the prediction benchmarks. With all 256 coordinates, boosted trees predict quantity levels better with EmbeddingGemma~2 than with the fine-tuned embeddings (58.77\% vs.\ 55.28\%) and price levels worse (55.54\% vs.\ 65.64\%). Five principal components or cluster similarities of EmbeddingGemma~2 retain much less of the price information than those of the fine-tuned embeddings (37.56\% and 32.23\% vs.\ 64.36\% and 62.53\%, compared with 18.54\% without embeddings).}

\paragraph{Homogeneous elasticities.} \revised{Figure~\ref{fig:gemma_ate} compares the homogeneous estimates across embeddings. With EmbeddingGemma~2 (text and image), the estimates of the specifications with embedding features differ from the fine-tuned ones by at most 0.030, less than one standard error. For Model I-3 with embeddings, the estimate is $-0.701$ (0.046), compared with $-0.697$ (0.049). The 768-dimensional variant gives similar estimates; the text-only variant gives less negative estimates in the boosted-tree specifications with $X^{e}$ ($-0.636$ for Model I-3 with embeddings). All 90\% intervals lie below $-0.5$.}

\paragraph{Heterogeneous elasticities.} \revised{Table~\ref{tab:gemma_chi2} reports joint tests of the cluster similarities as effect modifiers in Models II-1 to II-3. With EmbeddingGemma~2 (text and image), the similarities are jointly significant in all three models ($p$-values 0.001, ${<}\,0.001$, and 0.012, compared with 0.030, 0.054, and ${<}\,0.001$ for the fine-tuned embeddings), and the same holds for both variants at the 5\% level. The similarities refer to different clusters for each embedding. Figure~\ref{fig:gemma_sorted} compares the sorted effects. Without fine-tuning, they are more dispersed in the linear models, where the lower part of the curve is more negative (5\% quantile of Model II-1: $-1.17$ with EmbeddingGemma~2 and $-1.22$ text only, compared with $-1.08$). In Model II-3, they are less dispersed: less negative up to about the 80th percentile and slightly more negative in the upper tail (5\%, 50\%, and 95\% quantiles of $-1.28$, $-0.65$, and $-0.25$ with EmbeddingGemma~2, compared with $-1.33$, $-0.72$, and $-0.20$). With all embeddings, fitted responses span both sides of $-0.5$.}

\paragraph{Sensitivity analysis.} \revised{Table~\ref{tab:gemma_sensitivity} evaluates Model I-3 with embeddings at the residual shares of Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity_calibrated}. The upper confidence bounds are $-0.572$, $-0.537$, and $-0.502$ with EmbeddingGemma~2, compared with $-0.567$, $-0.533$, and $-0.498$. A negative effect is robust in all three scenarios, and elastic demand is robust at the calibration and its 1.5 multiple for both embeddings. At twice the calibration, the upper confidence bound lies marginally below $-0.5$ with EmbeddingGemma~2 and marginally above it with the fine-tuned embeddings (it reaches $-0.5$ at 3.11\% and 3.02\%). The observed-covariate benchmark of EmbeddingGemma~2 has a weaker alignment ($|\tilde\rho|=0.084$ vs.\ $0.150$) and implies a smaller calibration, $r_0=0.99\%$; at 1, 1.5, and 2 times this calibration, the upper confidence bounds are $-0.597$, $-0.575$, and $-0.552$.}

\revised{Overall, the homogeneous elasticities, the joint significance of the cluster similarities, and the robustness to omitted confounding are similar without fine-tuning; the sorted effects are somewhat more dispersed in the linear models and somewhat less dispersed in Model II-3. The embeddings differ mainly in their compressed features: the principal components and cluster similarities of the fine-tuned embeddings retain the price information and cut across product categories, whereas those of EmbeddingGemma~2 follow product categories and carry much less price information.}
